# Vigilant Crime — next long-form: British poverty (receipt format)

**Title (vidIQ 97):** Over 13 Million Britons Are in Poverty. Here's What a Week Looks Like
**Length:** about 4:00 at ElevenLabs pace (~620 words)
**Format:** one till receipt printing line by line; stat "stamps" land beside it; the final total is shown first.

## Voiceover (paste into ElevenLabs)

Minus twenty-two pounds.

That's not someone's bank balance. That's what's left at the end of a normal week, before they've paid a single penny of rent.

Over thirteen million people in the UK are living in poverty. So instead of telling you how bad it is, let's print it out.

Meet one person. Single. Over twenty-five. Out of work. On Universal Credit.

Line one. Money in.

The standard allowance this year is four hundred and twenty-four pounds ninety a month. Sounds like something you could live on, until you split it into weeks.

Ninety-eight pounds and five pence. That's the week. That's it.

Line two. Money out.

Two of the biggest anti-poverty organisations in the country, the Joseph Rowntree Foundation and the Trussell Trust, worked out what a single adult needs, at the very least, for food, household bills, clothes and the basics.

Not takeaways. Not subscriptions. The basics.

One hundred and twenty pounds a week.

So let's print the total.

Ninety-eight in. A hundred and twenty out.

Minus twenty-two pounds. Every single week. That's more than eleven hundred pounds a year that simply isn't there.

And rent isn't even on this receipt. That's a separate bill. The average private rent in the UK is now one thousand four hundred and six pounds a month.

Now multiply that receipt.

The Joseph Rowntree Foundation counted fourteen point two million people in poverty in 2023 to 24. More than one in five.

Six point eight million of them were in what it calls very deep poverty. The highest level in more than thirty years.

Three and a half million people in poverty can't afford enough food.

And here's the line that breaks the stereotype. Two thirds of working-age adults in poverty, five point four million people, live in a household where someone has a job.

Poverty in Britain isn't just people out of work. It's people at work.

Then there are the children.

Four and a half million in 2023 to 24. The newest government figures, using a new method, put it at around four million. That's more than one child in four.

Last year, food banks in the Trussell network handed out over two point six million emergency food parcels. More than nine hundred thousand of them were for children. That's the fourth year in a row above nine hundred thousand.

So is anything actually changing?

One line did change this year. In April, the two-child limit on benefits was scrapped. The Joseph Rowntree Foundation projects around four hundred thousand fewer children in poverty because of it.

That's real. But in October the energy price cap went up again. A typical home is now looking at around one thousand seven hundred and twenty-three pounds a year.

Here's my read. And it is just my read.

We talk about poverty like it's a number on the news. Fourteen million. Four million. It washes straight over you.

But it isn't a number. It's a receipt. It prints every week, and the total never changes.

Minus twenty-two. Before rent. Before the boiler breaks. Before Christmas.

You can argue about the fix. Benefits, wages, rent, all of it. But you can't argue with the maths.

So here's my question. If you had ninety-eight pounds for the week, what would you cut first?

Tell me in the comments. Every source is in the description.

This is Vigilant Crime. Subscribe for the next file.

## Check before recording

- Standard allowance: most sources give £424.90/month (single, 25+, 2026/27); MoneySavingExpert shows £424.95. Confirm on gov.uk; £424.90 × 12 ÷ 52 = £98.05. If it's £424.95, the week is £98.07 and the voiceover line changes.
- Children: JRF's 4.5M (2023/24) and the ~4M / 27% for 2024/25 (CPAG reading of DWP data) use different methods. The script says so; keep it that way.
- The 400,000 fewer children is a JRF projection, not a measured result.
- The £120/week essentials figure excludes rent; the script says so.
- Energy £1,723 is Ofgem's typical dual-fuel Direct Debit household, 1 Oct to 31 Dec 2026.
